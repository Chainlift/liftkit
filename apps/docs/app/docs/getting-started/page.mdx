# **The UI kit that lets you customize as much as you want while protecting you from hurting yourself**

LiftKit is my answer to the big question every design system tries to answer: how do you enforce aesthetic quality control while still giving users the freedom to be creative?

Most design systems are based on the following industry standard tenets:

- Base-4 pixel grid  
- As few tokens as possible  
- Linear scaling systems  
- Wholistic (if a pattern or component is not in this system, don't do it) 

I personally found this limiting, and I had no one around to tell me to just accept the things that bothered me as unavoidable facts of life.

For example, I ran into scenarios the above tenets couldn't address:


| Industry standard rule | Reason why | My problem with it | So I did this instead |
|-|-|-|-|
| Use a 4px grid | It's the only system intuitive enough to work at scale across large teams | I wanted spacing to be relative to content. Also I was a team of one, so I didn't care if anyone else could use my system. | Used em's for everything with no min or max increment |
| Use as few color tokens as possible | Makes it easier to use each token consistently | I needed all my projects to look very different | Reached for dynamic theming systems with rich, vibrant color sets |
| Scale things linearly by 1.25x | Matches with the 4px grid rule, looks good | Not perceptually accurate. The diff between 8px and 12px perceptually greater than that between 32px and 36px, even though it's the same interval.| Made a system that was proportional but asymmetrical |
| Be strict. If you find a case the system doesn't support, don't do it until the system is updated to support it. | It's the whole point of a design system. If designers aren't constrained, their visual styles will diverge over time, and the product loses its identity. | Getting changes approved is time-consuming, blocking, and requires human debate and review. I wanna go fast. | Sought a way to automatically check for aesthetic quality and consistency. 



There's a common theme you'll notice in what I was thinking at the time. This idea of automating aesthetic quality. And that's a pretty contentious point among designers. If you go snooting around looking for patterns in what makes beautiful things beautiful, you're going to find yourself immersed in pseudoscience. That's not all there is to it, of course. Reputable studies have shown that there are certain patterns in what humans find attractive, such as symmetry, sense of balance, etc. Furthermore, studies have also proven that some people do have sort of an innate sense for structure and logic. A common refrain among modern art critics is that their kids could paint that. But it turns out, if you show people abstract artwork made by children and animals, then show them works in similar styles by Pollock et al, about two-thirds of them can point out the one made by a real artist.



## Easy? No. Intuitive? Also no. You almost certainly do not need this.

## Aesthetic quality control baked into the architecture

LiftKit is a design system built on top of Base UI. It uses a unique scaling system based on relative units that ensures every single element is proportional to everything else. Think of it like a bowling lane with the bumpers up. You can’t hurt yourself.
